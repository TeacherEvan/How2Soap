# External Research Synthesis: Ancient Saponification & Archaeochemistry (2026 Baseline)

## 1. Archaeological Sources & Chronological Precision

1. **Sumerian Cuneiform Tablets (Girsu / Lagash, c. 2800–2500 BCE)**:
   - Found at Tello (ancient Girsu). Written in Sumerian cuneiform on baked clay cylinders.
   - Textual formulation: 1 liter of oil (usually sesame oil or rendered sheep tallow) mixed with 5.5 liters of potash/potassium alkali leached from halophytic or halite-associated saltwort ashes (*Salsola kali* / *Salicornia* / hardwood ashes).
   - Intended archaeological purpose: Scouring unwashed wool (*wool washing / degreasing*) prior to dyeing and carding, as well as therapeutic medicinal washes for skin lesions.

2. **Ebers Papyrus (Egypt, c. 1550 BCE)**:
   - Prescribes mixtures of alkaline salts (Natron / *ntry* - trona containing Na2CO3, NaHCO3, NaCl, Na2SO4) combined with animal and vegetable fats (goose fat, tallow, castor oil).
   - Applied topically to debride sores, treat parasitic skin infestations, and purify linen bandages.

3. **Pliny the Elder, *Historia Naturalis* (Book XXVIII, Section 191, c. 77 CE)**:
   - Term: *sapo*, derived from Celtic / Germanic root (*saipô*).
   - Quote context: "Prodest et sapo; Galliarum hoc inventum rutilandis capillis. Fit ex sebo et cinere, optimus fagino et caprino..." ("Soap is also useful; an invention of the Gauls for giving a reddish tint to hair. It is prepared from tallow and ash, the best from beechwood ash and goat's tallow, in two forms, thick and liquid; both are much used among the Germans...").
   - Typology: Noted distinction between soft soap (potassium soap from beech ash / *cinis faginus*) and hard soap (salted out or prepared with calcined lime / marine ash).

4. **Modern 2026 Bioarchaeological & Residue Analyses (FA-ORA / GC-MS)**:
   - Gas Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry (GC-MS) coupled with Compound-Specific Isotope Analysis (CSIA, $\delta^{13}\text{C}$ of palmitic (C16:0) and stearic (C18:0) fatty acids).
   - Distinguishes between adipose ruminant fat (beef/mutton tallow), non-ruminant fat (porcine lard), dairy fats, and vegetable oils (olive, sesame, linseed).
   - In ceramic sherd matrices, saponification leaves preserved insoluble fatty acid alkaline salts (metal-fatty acid complexes, e.g., calcium stearate diagenetic replacements and preserved potassium/sodium carboxylates).

## 2. Chemical Stoichiometry & Physical Chemistry Verification

1. **Quicklime Hydration & Causticization**:
   - $\text{CaO} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{Ca(OH)}_2$ ($\Delta H = -63.7\text{ kJ/mol}$, strongly exothermic).
   - $\text{K}_2\text{CO}_3 + \text{Ca(OH)}_2 \to 2\,\text{KOH} + \text{CaCO}_3\downarrow$ ($\Delta H = -17\text{ kJ/mol}$).
   - Calcium carbonate has minimal solubility ($0.013\text{ g/L}$ at $25^\circ\text{C}$), precipitating cleanly out of solution and yielding caustic potash lye (pH $\approx 13.5 - 13.8$).

2. **Triglyceride Saponification**:
   - Tristearin: $\text{C}_{57}\text{H}_{110}\text{O}_6$ ($M_r = 891.48\text{ g/mol}$).
   - $\text{C}_{57}\text{H}_{110}\text{O}_6 + 3\,\text{KOH} \to 3\,\text{C}_{18}\text{H}_{35}\text{KO}_2 \text{ (potassium stearate, soft soap)} + \text{C}_3\text{H}_8\text{O}_3 \text{ (glycerol)}$.
   - Stoichiometric mass ratio: $150\text{ g}$ tristearin ($0.1683\text{ mol}$) requires $0.5048\text{ mol}$ $\text{KOH}$ ($28.32\text{ g}$ $\text{KOH}$).

3. **Salting Out (Grain Soap / Hard Bar Formation)**:
   - $\text{C}_{18}\text{H}_{35}\text{KO}_2 + \text{NaCl} \to \text{C}_{18}\text{H}_{35}\text{NaO2}\downarrow + \text{KCl}$.
   - Addition of halite drives curding of insoluble sodium soap due to common-ion effect and lower solubility of sodium carboxylates relative to potassium carboxylates.
